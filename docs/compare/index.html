StreamSnag compared with Video DownloadHelper

The downloading most people use it for, free and with no limits. What costs money is what it doesn't do at all: turning a lecture into something you can study.

Checked against the other product's own public pages on 11 November 2026.

What this page compares

People who look for a way to save lecture videos usually find Video DownloadHelper first. It is a browser extension for saving web video, and by its own account it has been “used by millions for almost 20 years”. StreamSnag is new, and it is built for one job: saving lectures and studying from them.

Everything said below about Video DownloadHelper comes from its own public pages, read on 11 November 2026, and each row names its source. Its own pages write the name two ways, Video DownloadHelper and Video Download Helper; this page uses the first. Where we could not confirm something there, we left it out. If a row is out of date, write to support@streamsnag.app and we will correct it.

The short version

  • Choose Video DownloadHelper if you want to save video from many kinds of sites, if you use Firefox, or if you want nothing but the extension installed. It does things StreamSnag does not.
  • Choose StreamSnag if the videos are lectures. Saving them is free with no limit on streams, and Pro turns a saved lecture into a document you can read, a study guide, a quiz and flashcards.

What StreamSnag does not do

We do not claim StreamSnag does everything Video DownloadHelper does. From the table above:

  • It does not run in Firefox.
  • It does not download live broadcasts.
  • Some of its features need a helper program installed on your computer.
  • It has not been tried on anything like as many sites, over anything like as many years. It is built for course and lecture pages.
  • Its refund window is 7 days, which is shorter.

What costs money, in each

In Video DownloadHelper, the thing you pay for is more downloading: the free version limits streams, and Premium removes the limit. In StreamSnag, downloading is never limited. What you pay for is the study side, which a downloader does not have: transcription on your own computer, the reading edition, the study page, the study pack and review cards. Pro is US$39.97 a year, US$4.99 a month, or US$99 once for life. The study pack also needs an AI account of your own. AI use is never part of the StreamSnag price and is never paid to StreamSnag. With a Claude or ChatGPT subscription you already have it costs nothing extra; with a pay-as-you-go key it was between a few cents and about US$1.50 a lecture on our own runs, depending on the provider. Transcription and the reading edition need no AI at all.

If all you want is to save lecture videos, StreamSnag is free for that and you never need Pro.

And compared with online study-note tools

A downloader is one thing people compare StreamSnag with. The other is the kind of website or app that turns a recording into notes. This part describes that kind in general and names no product and no price of theirs: their plans differ and change, so check the one you are considering. We read the public plan pages of a few of them on 11 November 2026, and what follows is what those had in common.
