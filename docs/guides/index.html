Guides to saving and studying recorded lectures

Eight short guides, in the order you meet the problems: keeping the lecture, getting its words, reading it, and turning it into something to review.

  • How to download a lecture video you are allowed to keepFree, no AI account. Press play first, save the video with its slides and captions, and what to do when nothing shows up.
  • How to download all the lectures on a course pageFree, no AI account. Review the list before anything is saved: names, copies, previews, and what cannot be saved.
  • How to transcribe a lecture on your own computerFree from captions, Pro without them. From the captions when there are some, and by speech-to-text on your machine, with no minute limits, when there are none.
  • How to read a lecture instead of watching itPro, no AI account. Turn the recording into a document that keeps what was taught, with headings, a contents list and a minute beside every paragraph.
  • How to sync lecture slides with the recordingPro, no AI account. Match a PDF or PowerPoint deck to what is said, so every slide has its minute.
  • How to turn a lecture into flashcardsBy hand, or Pro with your own AI account. The rules of a good card in plain words, then two ways to make them, with Anki export.
  • How to make a study guide and practice quiz from a lecturePro, uses your AI. What a study pack contains, what your AI account is charged, and how to check what it wrote.
  • Studying from lectures without uploading themMostly no AI account. What runs entirely on your computer, and the one step that sends text.

Each one says which steps are free, which are Pro, and which use an AI account of your own.

Lecture one · PHYS201, lecture 6

Cardiac output and what sets it

An invented physiology lecture: 27 minutes, 9 slides, with captions.

  • Reading edition · No AI usedThe whole lecture as a document you read instead of watching: 3,194 words, a 14-minute read, with a time beside every paragraph.
  • Study guide · Written by AI5 key ideas, 2 review sheets, key figures, easily confused pairs, formulas and a glossary.
  • Flashcards and quiz · Written by AI9 flashcards and 8 questions, each with its minute, its slide and the lecturer's words.
  • Study page · No AI usedThe slides lined up with the transcript. This sample has no video, so choose a slide or a time and watch the other side follow.

The whole sample has 49 paragraphs in 4 parts, and its minutes open the sample study page, which has no video. The same lecture made without its slides: two of its four parts are headed by their own terms, two by number and minute. Also the flashcards and the Anki file.

Lecture two · Statistics for the life sciences, lecture 9

From a sample to a claim: standard error, confidence intervals and p-values

An invented statistics lecture: 28 minutes, 11 slides, with captions.

  • Reading edition · No AI used3,183 words, a 14-minute read, with two sentences marked as the lecturer's emphasis and five spoken lists set out as lists.

Lecture three · LAW101 Contract law, lecture 4

Offer and acceptance

An invented first-year law lecture: 23 minutes, 9 slides, with captions. Real landmark cases are named as any textbook names them; the words are our own.

  • Reading edition · No AI used2,706 words, a 12-minute read, with two sentences marked as the lecturer's emphasis (one is “this will be on the exam”) and four spoken lists set out as lists.
  • Study guide · Written by AI5 key ideas, 2 review sheets, easily confused pairs, a self-test and a glossary.
  • Flashcards and quiz · Written by AI14 flashcards and 6 questions, each with its minute, its slide and the lecturer's words.

Also the flashcards and the Anki file.

Which parts AI wrote. The study guides, quizzes and flashcards were written by AI from each lecture's captions and slide text, then checked against them. They can still be wrong, and each guide says so on its first screen. The reading editions and study pages were made without AI, by the current version of StreamSnag.

What the second pack took. The statistics pack was 20 AI requests in 1 minute 31 seconds and cost US$0.80 on Claude Sonnet. Of 65 statements pulled from the lecture, 61 passed the quote check. Of 17 flashcards written, 9 were kept.

What the third pack took. The law pack was made by StreamSnag itself, from its study desk: 21 AI requests to DeepSeek in under a minute, about US$0.04 at DeepSeek's peak-hours price.
