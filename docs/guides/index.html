Guides to saving and studying recorded lectures

Eight short guides, in the order you meet the problems: keeping the lecture, getting its words, reading it, and turning it into something to review. Each one says which steps are free, which are Pro, and which use an AI account of your own.

  • Free · No AI accountHow to download a lecture video you are allowed to keepPress play first, save the video with its slides and captions, and what to do when nothing shows up.
  • Free · No AI accountHow to download all the lectures on a course pageReview the list before anything is saved: names, copies, previews, and what cannot be saved.
  • Free from captions · Pro without themHow to transcribe a lecture on your own computerFrom the captions when there are some, and by speech-to-text on your machine, with no minute limits, when there are none.
  • Pro · No AI accountHow to read a lecture instead of watching itTurn the recording into a document that keeps what was taught, with headings, a contents list and a minute beside every paragraph.
  • Pro · No AI accountHow to sync lecture slides with the recordingMatch a PDF or PowerPoint deck to what is said, so every slide has its minute.
  • By hand, or Pro with your own AI accountHow to turn a lecture into flashcardsThe rules of a good card in plain words, then two ways to make them, with Anki export.
  • Pro · Uses your AIHow to make a study guide and practice quiz from a lectureWhat a study pack contains, what your AI account is charged, and how to check what it wrote.
  • Mostly no AI accountStudying from lectures without uploading themWhat runs entirely on your computer, and the one step that sends text.
