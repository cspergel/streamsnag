StreamSnag

Save your online lectures and study from them. Study packs use an AI account you choose.

AI provider

AI powers study packs, summaries, “Ask your AI to find it” (for a video StreamSnag can't see by itself), “Ask AI why” on a failed download, and tidier file names. It is off until you choose one of these, and nothing is sent until you use an AI action.

For study packs: a Google Gemini key works within Google's free daily allowance, so you can try them without paying. A Claude or ChatGPT subscription gives the fullest experience, with no bill for each lecture.

What gets saved

Videos you already have

StreamSnag looks through these folders (and their subfolders) to mark videos as downloaded. It matches by file size, length, the video's id in the name, or the video's own name, so renamed files and files saved by other tools count too. Your save folder and wherever StreamSnag saved before are always checked.

    Advanced

    File-name patterns, rules for one site, stream settings

    File names

    Words in braces are filled in for each download: {title} (the lecture/episode name found on the page) {number} and {name} (the two halves of a numbered title like "009: C. diff") {course} {page} (tab title) {quality} {host} {date} {kind}. Examples: {course} - {title}, {number} - {name}

    In a subfolder (above) you can use {site} {course} {date} {kind}, and / for nested folders.

    Rules for specific sites

    Use a different folder or file name on one site, e.g. a course site: folder Courses/{course}, name {number} - {name}. A rule for example.com also covers learn.example.com. Leave a field empty to use the general setting (a folder of . means no subfolder).

    Finding and downloading

    StreamSnag never downloads protected (DRM) videos. Only save what you have the right to keep.

    Backup

    Your settings, site recipes, site rules, download history and "downloaded" marks in one file, e.g. for a new PC.

    The file lists the pages and video links in your history and where the files are on your PC, so share it only with people you trust. Only import backups from people you trust, too.

    Site recipes

    A recipe tells StreamSnag where the video is on one website where it can't find it by itself, and, where the site says so, what the lecture is called and where its captions are. You never have to write one: when “Ask your AI to find it” finds a video, what worked is saved for that site by itself and used from then on (StreamSnag says so at the time, with an Undo). The ones you have are listed here, so you can remove one that has stopped working.

    A recipe is a short list of places to look on the page, never a program. It only applies to its own site, and the only other address it may ask is one on that same site.

    Diagnostics

    Turn this on if a video is missing or listed wrong, reproduce it, then copy the report into a bug report. It stays on your PC; it includes the page's media URLs, so check it before sharing.

    Local helper

    A small program your browser starts when it's needed. With it you can save into any folder, get one finished MP4 (video and audio merged with ffmpeg), download playlists and hard sites with yt-dlp, see which videos you already have, convert them, and use your Claude or ChatGPT subscription for AI.

    1. Open the StreamSnag folder you downloaded (the one that holds the extension folder).
    2. Windows: double-click Set up StreamSnag helper.cmd. It checks for Node.js and offers to install ffmpeg and yt-dlp.
      macOS / Linux: run sh setup.sh in that folder (it needs Node.js 18 or newer).
    3. Restart the browser, come back here and press Check helper.

    For AI with your Claude subscription, also run npm i -g @anthropic-ai/claude-code, then claude once to sign in. For your ChatGPT subscription: npm i -g @openai/codex, then codex login.
