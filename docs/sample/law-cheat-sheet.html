Offer and acceptance

23 min lecture
5 key ideas · 9 terms

Concept

How does the law decide whether two people have agreed, and why objectively?The law decides whether two people have agreed by using the pair of ideas offer and acceptance, which covers almost every case you will meet this year.01:44
When is a statement or display an offer rather than an invitation to treat?A statement is an offer when all the other side has to do is say yes and a contract is made; if it is just an opening move, it is an invitation to treat…03:41
How can an advertisement be a unilateral offer, and how is it accepted?An advertisement can be an offer, and Carlill v Carbolic Smoke Ball Company (1893) is the most famous contract case about an advert that was an offer.09:23
What does acceptance have to do to the offer, and what kills the offer?Acceptance has to match the offer exactly, which people call the mirror image rule: the acceptance is a mirror of the offer, a yes on exactly those terms.12:11
When is acceptance communicated, and when is silence or post enough?An acceptance generally has to be communicated; it is not enough to decide privately that you accept, and silence is not acceptance (Felthouse v Bindley, 1862).15:15

Definition

bilateral contractAn ordinary bilateral contract has promises on both sides: one promises to sell and the other promises to pay.11:01
counter-offerA counter-offer is a new offer the other side can accept or reject, and it kills the original offer so that it cannot be accepted later.12:39
invitation to treatAn invitation to treat is an invitation to make an offer, an opening move rather than a promise to deal with anyone who turns up.03:41
lapseAn offer lapses at the time it specifies, or after a reasonable time if it gives no time.19:54
mirror image ruleAcceptance has to match the offer exactly; this is called the mirror image rule.12:11
objective testThe law judges agreement objectively, asking what a reasonable person watching what you said and did would have understood you to mean.01:44
postal ruleWhere it is reasonable to accept by post, acceptance is complete as soon as the letter is posted, not when it arrives (postal rule).17:33
revocationA revocation of an offer takes effect only once it is communicated to the other side.20:26
unilateral offerDoing what was asked is the acceptance, and an offer of that kind is called a unilateral offer.10:33

Pitfall

Mistake: Thinking that all advertisements are invitations to treat and stopping there.

Instead: The general rule is only a starting point; an advert can be an offer, as in Carlill…08:18

Mistake: Thinking a unilateral offer cannot be made to the whole world.

Instead: The court held you can make an offer to the whole world, accepted by whoever does the thing asked.09:23

Mistake: Thinking acceptance must always be communicated to the offeror.

Instead: In a unilateral offer the offeror has, by its own words, done away with the need to be told…10:23

Mistake: Confusing unilateral and bilateral contracts.

Instead: In a unilateral offer only one side makes a promise; in a bilateral contract there are promises on both sides.10:43

Mistake: Thinking that privately deciding to accept is enough.

Instead: An acceptance generally has to be communicated; the offeror has to be told.15:15

Mistake: Thinking an offeror can turn silence into acceptance by saying so.

Instead: You cannot make somebody's silence into a yes just by saying so in your offer.16:25
